Ask an organization whether it is ready for AI and the answer usually describes technology: which platforms are licensed, which models are approved, which pilots are running.

Technology matters. But in our experience it is rarely the deciding factor. Organizations with modest tooling and strong foundations routinely outperform organizations with impressive tooling and weak ones.

We assess readiness across six dimensions.

## 1. Leadership and strategy

Is there a clear, shared view of why the organization is investing in AI, and what success looks like? Readiness here means named executive ownership, priorities linked to business outcomes and an agreed way of funding and evaluating initiatives.

**Warning sign:** AI activity is visible across the organization, but no one can explain how it adds up.

## 2. People and AI literacy

Do people understand AI well enough to use it with judgement — and are they encouraged to? Literacy is not the same as enthusiasm. It means knowing what AI does well, where it fails and how to check its work.

**Warning sign:** A small group of enthusiasts carries all the adoption, while most people are unsure what they are allowed to do.

## 3. Data and knowledge readiness

AI systems are only as reliable as the information they work with. This dimension covers data quality and accessibility, but also something often overlooked: whether institutional knowledge — policies, procedures, expertise — is documented, current and structured.

**Warning sign:** The most important knowledge in the organization lives in people's heads and inboxes.

## 4. Technology and infrastructure

Can the organization integrate AI into the systems it already runs, securely and at reasonable cost? This includes identity and access controls, integration capability and the ability to evaluate and monitor AI behaviour.

**Warning sign:** Every new AI idea requires a bespoke integration project.

## 5. Governance and responsible AI

Are there proportionate policies, clear decision rights and controls for privacy, security, fairness and accountability? Good governance accelerates adoption by telling people what is allowed. Poor governance either blocks everything or controls nothing.

**Warning sign:** People are using AI tools the organization does not know about — because asking for approval takes too long.

## 6. Process and operational readiness

Are workflows understood well enough to redesign? Are there baselines against which change can be measured? AI creates value by changing how work is done, and that is impossible if no one has mapped how work is done today.

**Warning sign:** Success stories are anecdotal, because nothing was measured before.

## Reading the results

Readiness is rarely even. Most organizations score well on one or two dimensions and poorly on others. The lowest-scoring dimension usually sets the ceiling on what AI can deliver — which makes it the most valuable place to invest next.
