Most organizations approach AI the way they approached their last major system rollout: define the scope, select a vendor, implement, go live, move on.

It is an understandable instinct. It is also why so many AI programs produce a burst of activity and very little lasting change.

AI is different in one important way. The technology improves faster than organizations can absorb it. What you implement this year will be surpassed next year — and the people using it will need to change how they work more than once. A one-time project cannot keep pace with that. A capability can.

## From project to cycle

The Praxis Method is the framework we use to build that capability. It has five stages, and the most important thing about them is that they repeat.

1. **Understand** — Build AI literacy and establish organizational context.
2. **Envision** — Identify opportunities and define strategic direction.
3. **Enable** — Develop the people, processes and technology capabilities.
4. **Execute** — Implement solutions and redesign workflows.
5. **Evolve** — Measure outcomes, learn and continuously improve.

Each pass through the cycle raises the organization's baseline. The second time through, *Understand* starts from a higher level of literacy. *Envision* draws on real evidence rather than vendor claims. *Enable* builds on foundations that already exist.

## Why each stage matters

**Understand** comes first because shared understanding is the cheapest risk control available. Leadership teams that disagree about what AI can do will disagree about everything that follows.

**Envision** is where most organizations under-invest in saying no. A good AI strategy is defined as much by the opportunities it declines as by the ones it pursues.

**Enable** is usually the real constraint. The model is rarely the problem. Data quality, unclear ownership, missing governance and untrained teams are.

**Execute** means changing real workflows, not deploying tools. We measure against a baseline from day one, because an initiative without a baseline cannot prove it worked.

**Evolve** closes the loop. What changed? What did people actually adopt? What did we learn that should change the next set of priorities?

> Knowledge creates potential. Praxis turns that potential into impact.

## Using the method

You do not need to start at stage one. Many organizations are already executing pilots without the understanding or enablement to scale them. The method is useful precisely because it shows where the gaps are.

A practical way to begin is to ask one question per stage, honestly:

- *Understand:* Does our leadership team share a realistic view of what AI can do for us?
- *Envision:* Can we name our three most important AI opportunities — and the ones we have deliberately set aside?
- *Enable:* Do we have the data, skills and governance to deliver those opportunities?
- *Execute:* Are our AI initiatives changing how work is done, measured against a baseline?
- *Evolve:* Do we systematically learn from what we deploy?

If the answers are uneven, that unevenness is your roadmap. Our [AI Readiness Assessment](/ai-readiness) is a structured way to find it.
